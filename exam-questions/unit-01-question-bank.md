# Unit 1 Question Bank: What Is Global Media?

## Definition and Recall

1. What is globalisation?
2. What is global media?
3. What is local content?
4. What is representation?
5. What is cultural identity?
6. What is media regulation?
7. What is platform power?
8. What is Western media?

## Explain Relationships

1. Explain the relationship between globalisation and cultural identity.
2. Explain the relationship between streaming platforms and local content.
3. Explain the relationship between representation and identity.
4. Explain the relationship between media regulation and cultural production.
5. Explain the relationship between platform power and local media industries.

## Compare

1. Compare global media and local media.
2. Compare cultural identity and national identity.
3. Compare local content and global content.
4. Compare platform power and government regulation.
5. Compare Western media and global media.

## Apply to Cases

1. Analyse how `Heated Rivalry` constructs a Canadian media identity.
2. Apply globalisation and local-content concepts to Canada's screen industry.
3. Explain how Netflix or another streaming platform can both support and constrain local production.
4. Use a media text to show how ownership, production, story, location and distribution may point to different identities.

## Discuss and Evaluate

1. Discuss whether globalisation supports or threatens local culture.
2. Discuss why local content remains important in global media.
3. Discuss whether national identity still matters in global media.
4. Discuss how streaming platforms affect national media industries.
5. Evaluate whether global streaming platforms help or harm local media industries.
6. Evaluate the usefulness of "Western media" as a category.
7. Evaluate whether media regulation is necessary in a global platform economy.

## Tutorial Questions

1. Discuss how production, ownership, story, location and distribution can construct the national identity of a media text.
2. Evaluate whether `Heated Rivalry` should be understood as Canadian, Western or global media.
3. Evaluate the usefulness and limits of classifying media as "Western".
4. Discuss why the classification of media cultures is shaped by power.

## Answer Pattern

Do not practise these as lists of facts. Every extended answer should include:

```text
Concept definition
-> direct answer
-> relationship or mechanism
-> case-study evidence
-> limitation or counterargument
-> concluding judgement
```
