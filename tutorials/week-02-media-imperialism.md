# Week 2 Tutorial: Media Imperialism

## Tutorial Purpose

The tutorial applies media and platform imperialism to contemporary cases and asks students to judge whether the paradigm remains relevant.

## Key Concepts and Clarifications

- Media imperialism focuses on media power; cultural imperialism is broader.
- Contemporary media imperialism is not always simply nation-based because TNCs and platforms operate across borders.
- The paradigm emphasises broad structures rather than isolated content.

### Cases Discussed

- Marvel Cinematic Universe and the US Pentagon
- *America's Army*
- Facebook blocking Australian news in 2021
- Netflix's most-watched shows
- exclusion of Francophone Africa from YouTube monetisation

These are tutorial prompts, not automatically proven examples. For each one, identify ownership, infrastructure, revenue, regulation, ideology and local consequences before applying the paradigm.

## How Should You Study?

For each case, identify who holds power, what resource they control, the resulting dependency and one limitation of the paradigm.

## Discussion Questions and Suggested Answers

### 1. Does the US exercise ideological influence in the global media industry?

**Answer:** Yes, but the influence is powerful rather than absolute. US corporations possess major advantages in production, distribution, technology and marketing, allowing American stories and values to circulate widely. Hollywood-Pentagon cooperation and *America's Army* illustrate how entertainment can normalise US military power, individualism and consumer capitalism. However, circulation does not guarantee acceptance. Audiences interpret media through local languages, identities and experiences, while successful Korean, Indian, Nigerian and other industries show that US ideology does not simply replace local culture.

**Judgement:** The US has disproportionate agenda-setting and representational power, but audiences and competing industries limit its effects.

### 2. Can local industries counter US media power?

**Answer:** Yes, although they do not compete from equal structural positions. Local industries can use culturally specific stories, languages and genres that foreign producers cannot easily reproduce. Public funding, local-content requirements, regional distribution and strong domestic audiences can also build production capacity. Canadian screen policy and the international success of non-US media demonstrate this possibility. Nevertheless, local producers may remain dependent on US-controlled platforms for global visibility, audience data and revenue, so cultural success does not necessarily remove economic dependency.

**Judgement:** Local industries can resist cultural domination, but overcoming ownership, infrastructure and distribution inequality is more difficult.

### 3. Has the world changed enough to make media imperialism irrelevant?

**Answer:** No. The original nation-to-nation and one-way-flow model is less complete, but its concern with concentrated structural power remains relevant. Media now moves through TNCs and platforms, while audiences have more choices and non-US industries have become globally successful. At the same time, a small number of corporations still control infrastructure, algorithms, advertising and data. Platform imperialism therefore updates media imperialism rather than making it obsolete.

**Judgement:** Media imperialism remains useful for analysing ownership and unequal flows, but it must be combined with audience agency, local culture and platform-specific mechanisms.

### 4. Is media imperialism limited to Western or US power?

**Answer:** No in principle, although US dominance is central to the paradigm's history. Imperialism describes an unequal relationship, not an identity that permanently belongs to one country. A non-Western state or corporation could exercise imperial power if it controlled infrastructure, capital, distribution and cultural visibility across less powerful markets. Chinese platforms and regional media corporations therefore deserve analysis. However, the existence of non-US firms does not automatically create an equal global order, because their reach, market access and geopolitical support must be compared rather than assumed.

**Judgement:** The framework should remain open to multiple centres of power while retaining evidence-based attention to current US advantages.

### 5. Can non-US platforms challenge US dominance and can local platforms survive?

**Answer:** They can challenge it, but survival depends on more than offering a good service. Network effects, large user bases, data accumulation, investment capital and established advertising systems protect dominant platforms. Local platforms may survive through language, cultural knowledge, specialised services, state protection or access to a large domestic market. They may still struggle to expand internationally or compete with firms that can subsidise entry into new markets. The decline of Korea's Cyworld after Facebook's expansion illustrates this structural disadvantage.

**Judgement:** Non-US platforms can become strong regional or national competitors, but challenging global US dominance requires capital, scale, policy support and durable local differentiation.

### 6. Can government regulation, policy and subsidies protect local industries?

**Answer:** Yes, but policy design and enforcement determine the result. Governments can fund domestic production, require investment in local content, support public broadcasters, regulate data and competition, and make foreign platforms contribute to national media systems. These measures can reduce market disadvantages and preserve cultural diversity. Yet platforms can lobby governments, threaten withdrawal, block services or use trade pressure. Facebook's blocking of Australian news in 2021 demonstrates this bargaining power.

**Judgement:** Government intervention is necessary for correcting structural inequality, but it is most effective when regulation, funding and competition policy operate together rather than as isolated measures.

## Answer Structure

```text
state a judgement
-> identify the structure of power
-> explain the mechanism
-> apply the contemporary case
-> test a limitation
-> refine the judgement
```

Workbook 1 requires comparison between media imperialism and cultural globalisation. The tutorial prioritises paradigm application, understanding the report's data and a persuasive judgement, not a descriptive report summary.
