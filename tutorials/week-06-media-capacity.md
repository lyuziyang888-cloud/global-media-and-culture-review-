# Week 6 Tutorial: Media Capacity

## Tutorial Purpose

Apply Keane's framework to companies and reconsider what counts as global media success.

## Key Concepts and Clarifications

- capacity requires repeatable skills, infrastructure and control
- one international hit does not prove lasting capacity
- regional success does not need to copy Netflix
- Keane's fifth category is titled **cultural/industrial milieu (creative clusters)** in the article
- global integration can create opportunities without removing concentrated ownership and unequal power

### Reading Framework

| Keane category | What it means | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| World factory | attracting outsourced production through lower costs and labour | foreign firms usually retain intellectual property; price competition can weaken local design and talent |
| Mimetic isomorphism | following or cloning a successful model under uncertainty | gives short-term returns but creates imitation, IP problems and diminishing returns |
| Cultural technology transfer | gaining training, expertise, infrastructure or shared IP through licensing, franchises and co-production | benefits can be short-term and local control may remain incomplete |
| Niche breakthrough markets | distinctive content grows from a niche into wider regional or international markets | a successful title does not by itself build an enduring production system |
| Cultural/industrial milieu | firms, workers, knowledge, infrastructure and institutions form a connected creative cluster | clusters cannot simply be declared by policy; they require genuine interaction, innovation and institutional support |

**Keane's central judgement:** The first four paths can achieve market success, but Keane states that they do not have a strong correlation with capacity-building. The fifth path offers the clearest basis for sustained capacity because knowledge, skills and production resources become locally connected.

## How Should You Study?

Learn each Keane category as a definition, one example and one limitation. Then practise judging whether a company controls valuable media capacity or only participates in global production.

## Discussion Questions and Suggested Answers

### Workbook 2 Discussion

These are discussion questions, not a tutorial exam-practice question.

### Which category appeared most often in your group?

**My answer:** My YRF case used niche breakthrough markets and cultural/industrial milieu. If I choose one, cultural/industrial milieu is more important because YRF has studios, post-production facilities and a visual-effects division within Mumbai's film cluster. Other companies may have similar infrastructure, but they differ in how much production capacity and intellectual property they control.

### Which strategy is most effective for becoming global?

Creative/industrial clusters are the strongest long-term strategy. A global hit may be temporary, while a cluster can support repeated production, knowledge exchange and innovation.

**Reading support:** Keane argues that the first four categories can produce success but are weak measures of capacity-building. A cultural/industrial milieu is stronger when specialised workers, firms, research, infrastructure and knowledge interact locally.

### Which company best demonstrates global media capacity?

**My answer:** Yash Raj Films best demonstrates global media capacity. It owns and produces Indian films, operates studio and post-production facilities, and distributes content internationally. *Pathaan* reached Russia and CIS territories on more than 3,000 screens. YRF is therefore no longer a peripheral producer, although it still uses Netflix and overseas market access to reach some audiences.

### Streaming-Service Discussion

### How should a successful streaming service be defined?

Success should not mean only competing with Netflix globally. A regional service may succeed by serving local audiences, languages and genres while expanding selectively into nearby markets.

### How does Keane challenge common ideas about streaming success?

Keane challenges the assumption that Western scale or one global hit is the universal standard. Regional success can create opportunities, but real capacity depends on what skills, intellectual property, institutions and production resources remain locally controlled. Services such as Viu should therefore be judged through regional influence and local capacity, not only by comparison with Netflix.

### What other standards could measure success?

Useful measures include local audience reach, control of intellectual property, sustainable production, regional influence and the ability to develop local talent.
