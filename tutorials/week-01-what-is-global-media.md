# Week 1 Tutorial: What Is Global Media?

## Tutorial Purpose

The tutorial introduces globalisation and tests the categories global, local, Canadian and Western against real media cases.

## Key Concepts and Clarifications

- Global and local are connected rather than simple opposites.
- Media nationality depends on several production, cultural and distribution dimensions.
- Geographical territory alone cannot determine whether media is local or global.
- Western media is a power-laden category rather than a neutral map label.

## Cases Discussed

- **Netflix keynote:** what does a platform mean when it calls media global, and is platform-led globalisation historically new?
- **Heated Rivalry:** its Crave origin, Nova Scotian source author and ice-hockey subject matter complicate American, Canadian, Western and global labels.

## Discussion Questions and Suggested Answers

### 1. What makes a media text Canadian?

**Answer:** Canadian identity can be established through creators, cast, crew, source material, funding, ownership, locations, cultural symbols and intended audience. No single criterion is sufficient. *Heated Rivalry* has Canadian industrial and cultural connections, but international distribution can also make it transnational.

**Judgement:** It is best described as Canadian-rooted media with global circulation.

### 2. How should local content be defined?

**Answer:** Local content is media substantially connected to a place through production, creative control, cultural reference, language, funding or audience. The label should not depend only on filming location because foreign firms can use a location without transferring meaningful control to local creators.

**Judgement:** Creative and industrial participation provide a stronger definition than geography alone.

### 3. What makes content Australian?

**Answer:** Australian content may involve Australian creators, stories, institutions, funding, locations, accents and cultural concerns. A production can still be Australian when foreign investment or distribution is involved, provided local creative and industrial participation remains substantial.

**Judgement:** Australian identity is multidimensional and can coexist with transnational ownership or circulation.

### 4. Is Heated Rivalry Western media?

**Answer:** It can be classified as Western because it emerges from Canadian and North American institutions and circulates through Western platforms. However, that broad label hides its specifically Canadian production and cultural identity.

**Judgement:** "Western" is technically possible but less analytically useful than a more precise Canadian and transnational classification.

### 5. Who is counted as the West and who is excluded?

**Answer:** The West usually centres North America, Western Europe and related settler societies, but its boundaries shift according to history, politics, race, economics and cultural power. The label can make dominant countries appear normal while treating other regions as peripheral.

**Judgement:** The category should be explained and questioned rather than treated as self-evident.

### 6. Can geographical territory alone explain global and local?

**Answer:** No. Media can be filmed in one country, financed in another, created by a transnational team and distributed globally. Geography remains relevant, but ownership, labour, story, regulation, platform control and audience must also be considered.

**Judgement:** Global and local are relationships produced across several dimensions, not fixed territorial opposites.

## Answer Structure

```text
define the category
-> choose relevant criteria
-> apply several production and cultural dimensions
-> identify ambiguity
-> make a justified classification
```
