# Week 8 Tutorial: Nation Branding

## Main Focus

Use Othering to examine who controls a national image and how foreign visitors and local people are represented.

## Workbook 3 Discussion

### 1. What nation branding campaign did you choose and what image was it trying to promote?

Visit Qatar presents Qatar as safe, luxurious and culturally interesting.

### 2. What Othering strategies did you identify in your videos, were all your group members finding similar scenes?

The videos centre foreign visitors, while local people mainly support their experiences.

### 3. Do you think nation branding or fictional content is an effective form of soft power? Can you come to an agreement as a group?

Nation branding gives Qatar more control over its image. Fiction may feel more natural and credible.

## Brisbane 2032 Activity

### 4. What are the three most important ideas (images) that represent Australia today?

A campaign could show multicultural Australia, Indigenous cultures and modern cities.

### 5. What would you avoid including? Why?

It should avoid stereotypes and using Indigenous culture without Indigenous control.

### 6. How is your version of Australia different from Sydney 2000?

It could focus more on diversity and Indigenous authority.

### 7. Are your choices based on what Australia is actually like, or what you think the world expects Australia to be like?

Both. Campaigns use real places but choose images that foreign audiences may expect.

### 8. Who gets to decide what represents Australia?

Governments and tourism organisations hold the most power.

### 9. Can a nation be represented without simplifying or falling into stereotypes?

Not completely. A campaign should avoid stereotypes and involve the communities it represents.

## How Should You Study?

Name the strategy, describe one scene and explain who acts, who is observed and who controls the image.
