# Format Programs and Globalisation

## Relationship

Format programs globalise production knowledge while local licensees adapt content and practices for specific audiences. They therefore create transnational interdependence rather than a simple choice between completely global and completely local television.

## Mechanism

```text
licensed format and production know-how
-> cooperation between licensor and local licensee
-> preservation of recognisable core elements
-> adaptation of hosts, language, humour, casting and setting
-> locally produced version within a transnational system
```

## Cultural Globalisation Interpretation

Formats demonstrate multidirectional circulation, local agency and cultural adaptation. Dutch-origin formats such as *1 vs 100* and *The Traitors* challenge the assumption that all globally influential television originates in the US.

## Media Imperialism Interpretation

Formats can still concentrate intellectual-property ownership, licensing revenue and rule-setting power in dominant firms. Local production may coexist with unequal control over the underlying format.

## Judgement

Formats strongly support cultural globalisation's account of adaptation and transnational exchange, but ownership and licensing must be examined before concluding that the relationship is equal.

## Possible Exam Questions

- In what ways has format trade made television more transnational?
- Do formats challenge West-versus-Rest thinking?
- Are format programs evidence of cultural globalisation or media imperialism?
