# The Unconscious and Identity

## Unit Role

**Unit 3 supporting concept.** Freud's approach challenges the assumption that identity is fully conscious and rational.

## 30-Second Definition

The unconscious refers to desires, memories and processes outside conscious awareness that can influence behaviour, identification and the development of identity.

## Keywords To Memorise

- Freud
- unconscious
- desire
- childhood
- identification
- limited self-knowledge

## Key Features

- People may not fully understand the motives shaping their behaviour.
- Childhood experiences can influence later identity.
- Identification with others contributes to psychological development.
- Speech and behaviour may reveal meanings that were not consciously intended.

## Why It Matters

The concept limits explanations that treat identity as a completely deliberate choice and adds a psychological dimension to social accounts of identity.

## Relationships

- The unconscious complicates [Identification](identification.md).
- It places limits on conscious agency within [Structure and Agency](structure-and-agency.md).
- Unintended signals can disrupt [Identity Performance](identity-performance.md).

## Example

A Freudian slip may reveal a desire or association that conflicts with the identity a person consciously attempts to present.

## Possible Exam Questions

- How does the unconscious influence identity?
- Why might identity not be fully conscious?
- Compare Freud's approach with Goffman's interactional approach.
