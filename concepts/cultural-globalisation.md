# Cultural Globalisation

## 30-Second Definition

Cultural globalisation is a paradigm containing theories and concepts that explain contextual, multidirectional and unequal interactions among media, people and cultures across borders.

## Keywords To Memorise

- multidirectional flows
- audiences
- meaning-making
- identity
- cultural proximity
- contra-flow
- media capitals
- change

## Key Features

- focuses on people, audiences, workers and everyday media practices
- treats cultures as diverse and changing rather than nationally uniform
- recognises local, regional and non-US production capacity
- examines movement in multiple directions
- emphasises context and interpretation

## Why It Matters

The paradigm explains phenomena that a simple one-way model misses: active audiences, culturally proximate preferences, non-US media success, adaptations and regional production centres.

## Relationships

- [Media Imperialism](media-imperialism.md) provides the contrasting structural paradigm.
- [Cultural Proximity](cultural-proximity.md) explains audience preference.
- [Contra-Flows](contra-flows.md) explains movement from former peripheries.
- [Media Capitals](media-capitals.md) explains concentrated non-state production centres.
- [Five-Scapes Model](five-scapes-model.md) describes overlapping global movements.

## Example

The adaptations of Venezuela's *Juana la virgen* demonstrate multidirectional movement and local meaning-making.

## Limitations

- can become abstract or overly celebratory
- may overlook ownership and unequal power
- may treat cultural diversity as proof of economic equality
- can unintentionally reproduce a market-friendly image of globalisation

## Possible Exam Questions

- What is cultural globalisation?
- Why is cultural globalisation important to global media studies?
- Compare cultural globalisation and media imperialism.
- Does multidirectional flow eliminate media dominance?
