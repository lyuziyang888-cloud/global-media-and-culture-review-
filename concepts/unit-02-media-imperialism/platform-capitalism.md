# Platform Capitalism

**Unit:** Unit 2: Media Imperialism
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

Platform capitalism is a business system in which privately owned digital platforms mediate interactions, collect user data and convert attention, activity and data into profit.

## How It Works

A platform supplies the technical environment in which users, advertisers and media producers meet. Because it owns that environment, it can set participation rules, record activity, rank content and charge for access to audiences. Network effects strengthen this position: as more people use the service, leaving it becomes more costly for users and organisations that need those audiences.

## Key Terms

- private infrastructure
- mediation
- users
- data extraction
- advertising
- commodification
- profit

## Concrete Course Evidence

- Facebook users communicate voluntarily, while the platform records attention and behaviour and sells targeted advertising.
- The users are internationally dispersed, but data ownership and advertising revenue remain concentrated in Meta.

## How The Evidence Applies

Posting, liking and sharing appear to be communication between users, but these actions also generate behavioural information and advertising inventory for Facebook. The platform does not need to produce every item of content; it profits by controlling the infrastructure through which other people communicate. The Australian news dispute further showed that publishers participate inside rules they do not independently control.

## Limitation

Platforms do not possess unlimited power. Governments can regulate them, users can move between services, and national or regional competitors may retain audiences through language and cultural knowledge. The concept identifies the business structure, not a guaranteed outcome in every market.


## Exam Use

**Possible long question:** Why are platforms not neutral intermediaries?


**Do not overclaim:** Corporate reach does not prove identical audience responses. Local industries, regulation and user practices can limit platform power.

**Course basis:** Week 2 lecture, limitations slides 27-28; Week 2 tutorial

## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> Why is Facebook not a neutral intermediary?

**Answer:** Facebook owns the environment where users, publishers and advertisers meet. It records activity, ranks content and sells access to audiences, so its rules and profit model shape the interactions it mediates.
