# Keane's Five Categories

**Unit:** Unit 6: Media Capacity
**Exam priority:** HIGH

## Definition

Keane (2006, pp. 836, 850-852) identifies five ways in which media industries in once-peripheral locations can enter global production:

1. **De-territorialisation (world factory):** production moves to a lower-cost location.
2. **Isomorphism (cloning culture):** producers imitate proven formats, genres or business models.
3. **Cultural technology transfer:** knowledge enters through co-production, licensing and franchising.
4. **Niche breakthrough markets:** culturally distinctive works reach markets beyond their original audience.
5. **Cultural/industrial milieu:** firms, workers, finance, infrastructure and knowledge form a connected local cluster.

## How Each Category Works

### 1. De-territorialisation

Foreign companies move labour-intensive work to cheaper production centres. Local workers may gain employment and technical experience, but the commissioning company can keep ownership, branding and the most profitable parts of the value chain. It therefore creates participation without necessarily creating local control (Keane, 2006, p. 850).

### 2. Isomorphism

Local producers copy products or organisational models that have already succeeded elsewhere. Copying lowers commercial risk and may help a new industry expand quickly. However, dependence on imitation and weak intellectual-property protection can discourage original development and prevent producers from building brands that generate continuing income (Keane, 2006, pp. 850-851).

### 3. Cultural Technology Transfer

Co-productions and licensed formats bring overseas finance, expertise and production methods into a local industry. Keane uses the partnership involving Warner Bros., China Film Group and Hengdian as an example. Chinese partners could learn international production practices, but the longer-term gain depended on whether that knowledge remained available to local producers after the project ended (Keane, 2006, p. 851).

### 4. Niche Breakthrough Markets

A locally distinctive product finds an international audience. Zhang Yimou's *Hero* and *House of Flying Daggers* showed that Chinese-language martial-arts films could travel widely. Their success increased the visibility of Chinese production, but one successful genre did not by itself create a complete and repeatable industry (Keane, 2006, p. 851).

### 5. Cultural/Industrial Milieu

A durable cluster forms when producers can repeatedly draw on nearby talent, suppliers, finance, facilities, knowledge and distribution networks. Regular interaction allows knowledge and skilled workers to circulate between projects. Keane argues that this can support continuing production and intellectual-property income, whereas numerous disconnected or duplicated industrial parks do not automatically create the same advantages (Keane, 2006, pp. 851-852).

## Comparison And Judgement

The categories are not automatic stages of development. A company may use several at once. The key distinction is between **access to global work** and **the ability to keep producing on local terms**. Outsourcing, copying, co-production and a successful niche can all provide opportunities, but their value depends on whether local firms retain knowledge, workers, infrastructure, ownership and future revenue. A functioning cultural/industrial milieu offers the strongest basis for repeatable capacity because those resources remain connected across multiple productions.

## Exam-Ready Application

If a company completes animation for a foreign studio but does not own the characters or distribution rights, it fits de-territorialisation: work has moved, but control has not. If the same company later develops its own intellectual property, trains workers across repeated projects and works within a cluster of local suppliers and distributors, it begins to show a cultural/industrial milieu. Keane's framework therefore evaluates not only whether media crosses borders, but also what productive resources remain in the local industry after circulation occurs.

## Lecture-Based Practice

**Question:** What is the difference between niche breakthrough and a cultural/industrial milieu?

**Answer:** A niche breakthrough occurs when a distinctive production reaches an international market, as *Hero* did. A cultural/industrial milieu is a continuing local system of skilled workers, firms, facilities and knowledge. The first proves that one product can travel; the second makes repeated production more likely.

**Source:** Keane (2006), especially pp. 836 and 850-852.
