# Keane's Five Models

**Unit:** Unit 6: Media Capacity
**Exam priority:** HIGH - memorise and practise applying it

## Definition

Keane identifies five models of global participation: the **world factory** relocates lower-cost production work; **mimetic isomorphism** copies successful products or formulas; **cultural technology transfer** shares expertise or intellectual property through licensing and co-production; **niche breakthrough markets** take distinctive content to wider audiences; and a **cultural/industrial milieu** connects firms, workers, infrastructure and knowledge in a local creative cluster. These models help distinguish international activity from lasting local capacity.

## What This Concept Explains

Keane's framework identifies five ways once-peripheral media industries may participate in and develop through the global economy.

## Key Terms

- world factory
- mimetic isomorphism
- cultural technology transfer
- niche breakthrough markets
- cultural/industrial milieu
- local capacity
- intellectual property
- creative cluster

## Concrete Course Evidence

- Indian animation outsourcing illustrates the “world factory”: it can create jobs and skills while foreign firms retain intellectual property.
- Hero and House of Flying Daggers illustrate niche breakthrough markets through wider distribution of culturally distinctive films.
- Seoul Digital Media City illustrates a creative/industrial cluster built from co-located infrastructure, firms and skilled labour.

## How the Evidence Shows the Concept

The categories are analytical models, not guaranteed stages. The fifth provides the strongest link to lasting capacity-building, while the others may still create opportunities and limitations.


## Exam Use

**Possible long question:** Which category best demonstrates lasting media capacity?

**Answer order:** definition -> relevant evidence above -> explain the connection -> limitation -> judgement.

**Do not overclaim:** Participation or market success does not automatically create lasting capacity. The key question is whether skills, infrastructure, knowledge and intellectual property remain locally controlled.
