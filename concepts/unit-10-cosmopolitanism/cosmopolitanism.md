# Cosmopolitanism

**Priority:** HIGH

## Definition And Source

Cosmopolitanism is openness to other cultures and a sense of connection to the wider world.

## Course Evidence

Ferreira interviewed 16 Spanish viewers of Turkish dramas. Some learned about Turkish customs through regular viewing; Isabella began studying Turkish; Lidia created a fan account, formed friendships and travelled to Istanbul (pp. 939-944).

## Application

The dramas placed unfamiliar practices inside everyday life. Evidence becomes stronger when viewers compare cultures, seek knowledge or form cross-border relationships. Viewing alone does not prove openness.

## Exam Use

## Official PPT Exam Practice

The Week 10 tutorial, slide 4, asks:

> Discuss how cosmopolitanism challenges cultural proximity using Spanish audiences watching Turkish dramas.

Define both concepts, contrast familiarity with attraction to difference, apply a Spanish-viewer example, and add the limitation that viewing alone does not prove cosmopolitan openness.

## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> Does watching Turkish drama automatically make a Spanish viewer cosmopolitan?

**Answer:** No. Viewing provides contact, but stronger evidence comes from reflection and action, such as Isabella learning Turkish or Lidia forming fan friendships and travelling to Istanbul.

**Long:** Separate access, routine exposure and active reflection. Note that the small, mostly female sample does not represent every Spanish viewer (pp. 938-939, 945-946).

**Source:** Ferreira (2026), pp. 932-949.
