# Media Imperialism

## 30-Second Definition

Media imperialism is a paradigm for explaining how media from powerful nations or corporations dominate other nations and cultures through unequal ownership, infrastructure, production, distribution and largely one-way flows.

## Keywords To Memorise

- unequal flows
- ownership
- domination
- core and periphery
- cultural influence
- political economy
- US power
- one-way flow
- local production

## Key Features

- Media power is concentrated in a small number of countries and corporations.
- Economic and technological dominance can support cultural influence.
- International media flows are structured by unequal resources and ownership.
- New technologies can renew rather than eliminate older power relations.
- Media imperialism is narrower than cultural imperialism because it focuses on the role and power of media.

## Why It Matters

The concept provides the historical framework for understanding why global communication does not automatically produce balanced exchange. It asks who owns the system, whose media travels, which values become normal and what happens to local production.

## Relationships

- [Platform Imperialism](platform-imperialism.md) extends the framework to digital infrastructure, data and platforms.
- [Globalisation](globalisation.md) can intensify unequal flows as well as connection.
- [Western Media](western-media.md) raises related questions about whose institutions dominate global media.
- [Dependency Theory](dependency-theory.md) explains how unequal relationships can persist after formal colonialism.
- [World-Systems Theory](world-systems-theory.md) describes the core, semi-periphery and periphery structure.
- [One-Way Media Flow](one-way-media-flow.md) is a central mechanism.

## Example

The lecture uses Hollywood, Disney, US news agencies and Pentagon-linked entertainment as traditional examples. Jin presents Facebook as evidence that dominance continues in a platform-based form.

## Major Concerns

- US control over global information flows
- cheap foreign programs crowding out local production
- foreign production and technical standards becoming dominant
- capitalist consumer values and US political ideology travelling with media
- cultural homogenisation and reduced diversity

## Limitations

- Audience effects are uncertain and audiences actively interpret media.
- National labels can hide internal diversity.
- US content is not always dominant in overseas markets.
- The paradigm struggles to explain successful non-US industries and content.

## Possible Exam Questions

- What is media imperialism?
- How is platform imperialism different from traditional media imperialism?
- Does the rise of non-Western platforms disprove media imperialism?
- Is media imperialism still relevant today?
