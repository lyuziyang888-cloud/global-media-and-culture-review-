# Asymmetrical Interdependence

**Unit:** Unit 3: Cultural Globalisation
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

Asymmetrical interdependence is mutual dependence characterised by unequal power between the parties involved.

## Mechanism

Straubhaar's position, discussed by Mirrlees (2013, Chapter 1), rejects both total dependence and equal exchange. National industries, states and audiences can influence what circulates, but they operate from unequal positions. The relationship is interdependent because both sides need resources from the other; it is asymmetrical because finance, ownership, bargaining power or distribution remain uneven.

## Key Terms

- connection
- mutual dependence
- inequality
- influence
- complexity
- power

## Concrete Course Evidence

- Global platforms need culturally specific stories, labour and audiences from local industries.
- Local producers may need platform investment, data and international distribution.
- India later provides a concrete course case: Netflix adapts to Bollywood while Indian services adopt global streaming practices.

## How The Evidence Demonstrates It

Neither side is inactive. Local industries shape the content and market strategy that a global platform can use, while the platform controls resources and routes that local producers may struggle to replace. Mutual influence therefore exists without equal control. This is the middle ground between treating local producers as powerless and treating cross-border exchange as flat.


## Exam Use

**Possible long question:** How does it provide a middle ground between the two paradigms?

**How to answer:** Identify what each side needs from the other, then compare their control over money, rights, data and distribution.

**Do not overclaim:** Multidirectional circulation and active audiences do not remove unequal ownership, platform control or distribution power.

**Course basis:** Mirrlees (2013), Chapter 1, discussion of Straubhaar; Week 3 lecture, slides 9-12.

## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> Can global platforms and local producers depend on each other unequally?

**Answer:** Yes. Platforms need local stories, talent and audiences, while producers need finance and distribution. They are interdependent, but platforms may retain greater control over data, intellectual property and market access.
