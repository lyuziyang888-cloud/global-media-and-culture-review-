# Globalisation

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** CORE - know the definition and one case

## Definition

In this unit, **globalisation** means the processes through which media production, ownership, distribution and audiences become organised across national borders. This working definition comes from the Week 1 lecture and tutorial. The assigned Stevis-Gridneff (2026) reading supplies a case for applying the concept; it is not presented as the source of a general textbook definition.

## How It Works

Media becomes global through several different cross-border links. Finance may come from one country, creators and filming from another, and distribution from an international platform. These links do not make the national or local level disappear. They connect local production to institutions that can expand its audience while also controlling important routes to that audience.

## Concrete Course Evidence

- ***Heated Rivalry*:** The project originated with Canadian platform Crave. Its Nova Scotian source author, Canadian creators and production personnel, hockey subject matter, locations and imagery establish Canadian connections. Distribution through HBO then carries that production into the US market (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).
- ***North of North*:** The series is set in a Canadian Arctic Inuit community and involves Indigenous creators, performers and production personnel. CBC and APTN connect it to Canadian and Indigenous broadcasting institutions, while Netflix distributes it internationally (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).
- **Week 1 tutorial method:** The tutorial instructs students to assess media nationality through source material, creators, cast, crew, ownership, funding, location, story and distribution rather than selecting one feature.

## How The Cases Demonstrate Globalisation

In *Heated Rivalry*, globalisation occurs at the **distribution stage**: a production with identifiable Canadian creative and cultural inputs reaches the United States through HBO. HBO's involvement does not retroactively make the source author, hockey references or Canadian labour American. The case therefore shows cross-border circulation connecting national industries rather than automatically replacing local identity.

In *North of North*, Netflix provides an international route for a story grounded in an Inuit community and Indigenous-led production. This can increase access to the program outside Canada, but Netflix's role also means part of the route to global audiences is controlled by a transnational platform. The same arrangement can therefore increase cultural visibility and create distribution dependence.

The two cases show the mechanism more precisely: **local creative resources produce cultural specificity; national institutions support production; global platforms extend circulation; control is divided across those stages.**


## Exam Use

**Possible long question:** Explain the relationship between globalisation and local culture.

**How to answer:** Identify the exact cross-border connection first. Then separate production, cultural identity and distribution instead of calling the whole text simply “global.” Finish by explaining who gains reach and who retains control.

**Do not overclaim:** Global circulation does not automatically erase local identity, but visibility through a global platform does not give local producers full control over funding or circulation.

**Course basis:** Week 1 lecture and tutorial; Stevis-Gridneff (2026), PDF pp. 1-2.

## Possible Short Answer

> What is Globalisation? Give one course example.

**Answer:** Globalisation organises media production, ownership, distribution or audiences across borders. *North of North* combines an Inuit setting and Indigenous Canadian production with international Netflix distribution. The local production and global distribution are connected, but they are not the same form of control.
