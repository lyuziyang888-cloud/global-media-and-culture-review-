# Media Regulation

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

Media regulation refers to government rules, policies and institutions that shape how media industries operate, fund content and serve public or cultural goals.

## Mechanism

Regulation changes the conditions under which media companies enter a national market. Funding rules and platform levies can redirect part of the revenue earned from local audiences into domestic production. Regulation therefore addresses an industrial imbalance: global distributors can profit from a market without automatically financing stories or producers located there.

## Key Terms

- policy
- government
- funding
- local content
- public interest
- cultural protection
- platform obligations

## Concrete Course Evidence

- The CRTC proposed a 15% levy on major streamers’ Canadian revenue.
- The proposed revenue would support domestic and Indigenous production.

## How The Case Demonstrates It

The CRTC proposal would require major streamers to contribute 15% of Canadian revenue to domestic and Indigenous production. The percentage is the regulatory instrument; supporting local production is its intended industrial and cultural outcome. The political decision to block the proposal also shows that regulation is contested rather than an automatic national response (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).


## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> Why might Canada regulate foreign streaming platforms?

**Answer:** Foreign platforms earn revenue and influence access to Canadian audiences. Funding obligations and content rules try to return resources to Canadian and Indigenous production and protect cultural goals that the market may not fund by itself.

## Possible Long-Answer Practice

> (a) What problem was Canada's proposed streaming levy intended to address? (b) How was the levy supposed to operate? (c) What does the dispute reveal about regulation and platform power?

- **(a):** Global streamers can earn money from Canadian audiences without automatically directing an equivalent share toward Canadian and Indigenous production.
- **(b):** State the reading's example: the CRTC proposed requiring major streamers to contribute 15% of Canadian revenue to domestic and Indigenous production.
- **(c):** Explain that the proposal attempted to redistribute platform revenue toward local cultural production, while its political blockage shows that governments, platforms and domestic producers contest who should finance that production.
