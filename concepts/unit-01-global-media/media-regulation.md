# Media Regulation

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

Media regulation refers to government rules, policies and institutions that shape how media industries operate, fund content and serve public or cultural goals.

## What This Concept Explains

Media regulation refers to government rules, policies and institutions that shape how media industries operate, fund content and serve public or cultural goals.

## Key Terms

- policy
- government
- funding
- local content
- public interest
- cultural protection
- platform obligations

## Concrete Course Evidence

- The CRTC proposed a 15% levy on major streamers’ Canadian revenue.
- The proposed revenue would support domestic and Indigenous production.

## How the Evidence Shows the Concept

Media regulation matters because global media markets do not automatically protect local culture, national identity or minority representation.


## Exam Use

**Possible long question:** Why do governments regulate global streaming platforms?

**Answer order:** definition -> relevant evidence above -> explain the connection -> limitation -> judgement.

**Do not overclaim:** Global circulation does not automatically erase local identity, but visibility through a global platform does not give local producers full control over funding or circulation.

**Course basis:** Week 1 tutorial; Unit 1 evidence map
