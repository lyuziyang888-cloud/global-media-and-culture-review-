# Cultural Identity

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

Cultural identity refers to the shared meanings, values, symbols, stories and practices through which a group understands itself.

## Mechanism

Cultural identity is produced when particular stories, places, symbols and voices are repeatedly associated with a community. Media participates by selecting who speaks for the community and which experiences become visible. The Week 1 materials apply this idea through production and authorship rather than supplying a separate identity theory.

## Concrete Course Evidence

- The series is set in an Inuit community in the Canadian Arctic.
- Indigenous Canadians have substantial roles in its creation, performance and production.
- CBC and APTN connect it to Canadian and Indigenous media institutions; Netflix gives it international distribution.

## How The Case Demonstrates It

*North of North* is set in an Inuit Arctic community and involves Indigenous Canadians in creation, acting and production. These are not interchangeable details: the setting supplies culturally specific social space, while Indigenous participation gives members of the represented community influence over the story and performance. Netflix expands circulation, but it is not the source of that cultural authorship (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).


## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> How does *North of North* express cultural identity?

**Answer:** Its Inuit setting, Indigenous creators and community-centred storytelling allow people connected to that culture to shape how it appears. The example links cultural identity to authorship, not only to scenery.

## Possible Long-Answer Practice

> (a) Explain how media contributes to cultural identity. (b) Apply this idea to *North of North*. (c) Does Netflix's global distribution weaken the program's local identity?

- **(a):** Media associates stories, symbols, places and voices with a community and helps make that community visible.
- **(b):** Identify the Inuit Arctic setting, Indigenous creators and performers, and the roles of CBC and APTN. Explain that identity comes from both what appears on screen and who participates in producing it.
- **(c):** Argue that Netflix changes the scale of circulation, not the origin of the program's cultural authorship. It may create distribution dependence, but the available evidence does not show that the Inuit identity has been erased.
