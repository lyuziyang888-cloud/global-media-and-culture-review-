# Local Content

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** CORE - know the definition and one case

## Definition

Local content refers to media that is connected to a particular place, culture, community or nation through its production, stories, creators, locations, language or themes.

## How To Identify It

The Week 1 tutorial rejects location as a sufficient test. Assess source material, creative decision-makers, cast and crew, funding, ownership, story, cultural references and intended audience. A foreign production using a Canadian location may create local employment without giving Canadians meaningful creative control.

## Concrete Course Evidence

- The project originated with Canadian platform Crave.
- Its source author Rachel Reid is from Nova Scotia, and its ice-hockey subject matter carries Canadian cultural associations.
- Canadian creators, cast, crew, locations and imagery make it locally identifiable, while HBO provides US circulation.
- The series is set in an Inuit community in the Canadian Arctic.
- Indigenous Canadians have substantial roles in its creation, performance and production.
- CBC and APTN connect it to Canadian and Indigenous media institutions; Netflix gives it international distribution.
- The Week 1 tutorial requires nationality to be assessed through source material, creators, cast, crew, funding, ownership, locations, story and distribution.
- A production can therefore be locally rooted and globally circulated at the same time.

## Application To The Cases

*Heated Rivalry* has several independent Canadian connections: it originated with Crave, adapts work by Nova Scotian author Rachel Reid, uses hockey as a cultural reference and involves Canadian personnel and locations. HBO distribution adds a transnational layer but does not remove those inputs. *North of North* has a stronger community-based claim because its Inuit setting is joined by Indigenous creative and production participation. The comparison shows why local content is established by a combination of industrial and cultural evidence, not one flag or filming site (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).


## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> Is content local only because it is filmed locally?

**Answer:** No. Local content may be identified through creators, stories, language, cultural markers, funding and locations. *North of North* is local because Indigenous authorship and Arctic community representation shape the production, not merely because of its setting.

## Possible Long-Answer Practice

> (a) Explain why filming location alone cannot define local content. (b) Compare the local-content claims of *Heated Rivalry* and *North of North*. (c) Which case gives the represented community stronger creative involvement?

- **(a):** A foreign production can use a location and employ workers while ownership, story and creative decisions remain elsewhere. Source material, creators, funding, institutions and cultural references must also be considered.
- **(b):** Use Crave, Rachel Reid, hockey, Canadian personnel and locations for *Heated Rivalry*. Use the Inuit setting, Indigenous creative and production participation, CBC and APTN for *North of North*.
- **(c):** Select *North of North* because the evidence links cultural representation to Indigenous participation in creation, performance and production, not only to national symbols or location.
