# National Identity

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** SUPPORTING - recognise it and use it when relevant

## Definition

National identity refers to the shared stories, symbols, values and cultural markers through which people imagine themselves as part of a nation.

## Mechanism

Media can make a nation recognisable by repeatedly selecting particular landscapes, institutions, accents, histories and cultural practices. The Week 1 tutorial also treats national identity as contested: ownership, creators, story and distribution may point to different countries, so nationality must be argued rather than read from one production label.

- CBC and APTN connect it to Canadian and Indigenous media institutions; Netflix gives it international distribution.
- The Week 1 tutorial requires nationality to be assessed through source material, creators, cast, crew, funding, ownership, locations, story and distribution.
- A production can therefore be locally rooted and globally circulated at the same time.

## How The Case Demonstrates It

*Heated Rivalry* is identified as Canadian through a cluster of evidence: Rachel Reid's Nova Scotian authorship, hockey, Canadian production personnel, locations and imagery. HBO gives the program US circulation, but circulation is not the same as the cultural and industrial sources of the text. The case shows how national identity can remain visible inside transnational distribution (Stevis-Gridneff, 2026, pp. 1-2).
