# Global Media

**Unit:** Unit 1: Global Media
**Exam priority:** CORE - know the definition and one case

## Definition

Global media refers to media content, industries, platforms and audiences that operate across national and cultural boundaries.

## How To Identify Global Media

The Week 1 lecture treats “global” as a conclusion that must be demonstrated, not a label based only on international popularity. Examine where finance, ownership, creative labour, story, filming, distribution and audiences are located. A text becomes part of global media when these activities connect institutions or audiences across borders, even when its story and production remain strongly local.

## Concrete Course Evidence

- The project originated with Canadian platform Crave.
- Its source author Rachel Reid is from Nova Scotia, and its ice-hockey subject matter carries Canadian cultural associations.
- Canadian creators, cast, crew, locations and imagery make it locally identifiable, while HBO provides US circulation.
- The series is set in an Inuit community in the Canadian Arctic.
- Indigenous Canadians have substantial roles in its creation, performance and production.
- CBC and APTN connect it to Canadian and Indigenous media institutions; Netflix gives it international distribution.
- The Week 1 tutorial requires nationality to be assessed through source material, creators, cast, crew, funding, ownership, locations, story and distribution.
- A production can therefore be locally rooted and globally circulated at the same time.

## How The Cases Demonstrate It

*Heated Rivalry* links Canadian source material, labour and cultural references to US circulation through HBO. It is global at the level of distribution without losing all Canadian production identity. *North of North* similarly connects Inuit representation and Canadian institutions to Netflix's international network. These examples demonstrate that a media text can contain local cultural authorship, national industrial support and transnational circulation at the same time.

The cases also reveal unequal roles. Local creators supply the culturally specific story, while HBO or Netflix controls an important route to audiences outside Canada. “Global media” therefore describes both cross-border connection and the division of power within that connection.


## Derived Short-Answer Practice

> How can *Heated Rivalry* be both Canadian and global?

**Answer:** Its creators, locations and cultural markers connect it to Canada, while international platform distribution takes it beyond Canada. Production identity and circulation therefore operate at different scales.

## Possible Long-Answer Practice

> (a) What makes a media text global? (b) Explain how *Heated Rivalry* is simultaneously Canadian and global. (c) What does this case reveal about power in global media?

- **(a):** Show cross-border links in finance, ownership, labour, story, distribution or audiences; international popularity by itself is not the whole test.
- **(b):** Use its Canadian source author, hockey associations, creators, personnel, locations and Crave origin, then identify HBO as the route into the US market.
- **(c):** Conclude that local producers can retain cultural and production identity while an international distributor controls an important route to foreign audiences. Global connection does not mean equal control.
