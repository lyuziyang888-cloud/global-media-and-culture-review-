# Week 1 Lecture: Course Overview

## Central Purpose

The lecture introduces global media as a field of critical inquiry, explains how the unit operates and shows why media nationality cannot be determined by a single label.

## Main Learning Direction

- understand media industries and cultures beyond the US, UK and Western Europe
- examine different implications of globalisation
- question simple uses of "global," "local" and "Western"
- develop critical thinking rather than rely on common-sense media knowledge

## Opening Case: KPop Demon Hunters

The question "Is this film American?" should be analysed through several dimensions:

- cast
- crew
- plot
- genre
- filming location
- production and ownership
- distribution and audience

The case introduces a recurring course principle: media identity can be hybrid and contested.

## How The Unit Works

- Lectures provide background and develop theories, concepts and ideas; they do not replace readings.
- Tutorials assume completion of lectures and readings and are used to practise critical thinking.
- Assessment 1 contains four 500-word pieces.
- Tutorial engagement is assessed weekly from Week 2, with the two lowest scores dropped.
- The final exam is worth 60 percent.

## Course Questions Previewed

- How should globalisation be understood?
- How do formats, regional streaming services and media firms complicate global and local?
- Does the nation still matter?
- How do soft power and nation branding operate?
- What can anime, Turkish drama, K-pop, Bollywood and Nollywood teach us?
- What counts as the West, and why?
