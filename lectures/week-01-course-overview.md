# Week 1 Lecture: Course Overview

## Central Purpose

The lecture introduces global media as a field of critical inquiry, explains how the unit operates and shows why media nationality cannot be determined by a single label.

## Lecture Structure

1. question the nationality of *KPop Demon Hunters*
2. explain why studying global media matters
3. introduce critical thinking as the required learning method
4. explain lectures, tutorials and assessments
5. preview the course's major themes
6. identify Week 2 preparation and readings

## Key Concepts and Definitions

- **Global media:** media industries, texts and flows that operate across borders while remaining shaped by national and local conditions.
- **Critical thinking:** analysing, evaluating and creating arguments rather than only remembering information.
- **Media nationality:** a classification built from several dimensions, including cast, crew, story, genre, location, ownership and distribution.
- **Western-centred thinking:** treating the US, UK or Western Europe as the normal centre of media analysis.

## Main Arguments

- Global media study should de-centre the US, UK and Western Europe.
- Terms such as globalisation and global media conceal real complications.
- Media identity can be hybrid and contested.
- Common-sense media knowledge is insufficient for passing the unit.
- Lectures, tutorials and readings perform different but connected roles.

## Cases and Evidence

### KPop Demon Hunters

The question "Is this film American?" should be analysed through cast, crew, plot, genre, filming location, production, ownership, distribution and audience. The case introduces a recurring course principle: no single national label captures every dimension of a media text.

## Assessment and Exam Signals

- Lectures develop theories, concepts and background; they do not replace readings.
- Tutorials assume completion of lectures and readings and are used to practise critical thinking.
- Assessment 1 contains four 500-word pieces.
- Tutorial engagement is assessed weekly from Week 2, with the two lowest scores dropped.
- The final exam is worth 60 percent.
- Course themes include globalisation, global/local relations, regional platforms, media firms, nation branding, soft power and non-Western industries.
- Week 2 readings shown on slide 13 are Mirrlees Chapter 1, assigned pages 19-36, and Jin Chapter 13.
