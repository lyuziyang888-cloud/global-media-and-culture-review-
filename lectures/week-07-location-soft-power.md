# Week 7 Lecture: Location as Soft Power

## Focus

The lecture examines how governments, media-service companies and intermediaries construct places for foreign production, using Budapest as the main case.

## Key Sequence

1. Soft power shapes behaviour through attraction rather than coercion or payment. The reading extends its target from general audiences to foreign media professionals.
2. Places are not simply natural or fixed; media industries help imagine and produce their meanings.
3. Media-service industries support productions made by outside companies and are often called runaway production industries.
4. Budapest attracts foreign productions through adaptable locations, skilled workers, facilities and tax incentives.
5. Hungary promotes national stories while also presenting Budapest as a flexible destination for foreign production.
6. Fixers connect government, foreign producers and local stakeholders, translating Budapest into locations that can represent other places.
7. Spy thrillers suit Budapest because Eastern Europe's geopolitical image and the city's versatility allow it to appear both distinctive and interchangeable.
8. Service production can provide investment, work and expertise, but foreign firms can relocate and Hungary still has limited international reach as a creator of its own media.

## Reading Evidence

- foreign production investment grew nearly 500% between 2018 and 2024
- almost US$1 billion is spent on film production in Hungary each year
- qualifying productions can receive a 30% rebate on direct Hungarian film costs
- *The Day of the Jackal* spent more than US$125 million overall; most production took place in Hungary
- Budapest represented Paris, Munich and Istanbul while also appearing as itself

These figures come from Havens and Imre, "Location as Soft Power in the Media Services Industry: The Case of Hollywood East," pp. 25-41.

## Key Actors

| Actor | Role in place-making |
|---|---|
| Hungarian government and NFI | use funding, festivals and film diplomacy to promote national and production-friendly images |
| Fixers | reassure foreign companies, coordinate local relationships and translate the city into usable production locations |
| Foreign producers | select and frame locations according to genre, cost and production needs |
| Local workers and facilities | provide the expertise that makes Budapest a credible service-production centre |

## Lecture Questions

- What is soft power, and how can a place be made?
- Why is Budapest an unfixed or chameleon location?
- How do the Hungarian government and local fixers perform different roles?
- Why do spy thrillers work particularly well in Budapest?
- What benefits and limitations can media-service industries create?
