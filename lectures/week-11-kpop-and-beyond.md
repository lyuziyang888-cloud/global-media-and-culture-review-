# Week 11 Lecture: The Possibilities of K-pop

## Central Purpose

The lecture uses cultural hybridity and transculturalism to explain how K-pop industries and fans operate across cultural and national boundaries.

## Lecture Structure

1. define K-pop
2. introduce transculturalism and cultural hybridity
3. examine cultural hybridity in K-pop production
4. explain K-pop's global industrial strategies
5. examine cover dances, lightsticks and fan chants
6. question what the "K" in K-pop means

## Key Concepts and Definitions

- **K-pop:** Korean popular music associated mainly with dance music, idol groups, performance and the entertainment-agency trainee system.
- **Transculturalism:** an approach to fandom that does not use nationality as its only explanation. Fans from different backgrounds form communities through shared interests and affective affinity.
- **Cultural hybridity:** the mixing of distinct forms, styles and identities to create a different kind of culture. It can challenge fixed ideas about cultural origins.
- **Cultural discount:** the reduced overseas value or appeal that a cultural product may experience because it was made in a particular national context.
- **Participatory globalisation:** fans participate in and play with the objects of their fandom.
- **Cosmopolitan globalisation:** fans consider the inclusiveness of K-pop and globalisation from perspectives beyond the West.
- **Cute globalisation:** fans understand K-pop idols as softer and more approachable than Western singers.

## Main Arguments

- K-pop includes music, performance and visual culture rather than music alone.
- YouTube, TikTok and fan services support K-pop's visual strengths and participatory culture.
- K-pop uses foreign members and creative workers, multilingual idols, English lyrics and multilingual releases to reach international audiences.
- Cultural hybridity helps K-pop reduce cultural discount because audiences can recognise several musical and cultural influences.
- Fans reinterpret K-pop through cover dances and reuse fan objects in new cultural and political settings.
- Transculturalism explains fandom through shared interests and affinities that cross national boundaries.
- K-pop's changing performers and technologies make the meaning of "Korean" increasingly difficult to define through ethnicity or nationality alone.

## Cases and Evidence

- ***Lalisa*:** Teddy Park and Bekuh Boom wrote the song, while choreographers from New Zealand, the United States and South Korea contributed to the performance.
- **Cover dance in Laos:** Mfrolic Academy's *Lalisa* cover shows how fans reinterpret a K-pop performance in another location.
- **Lightsticks in Chile and South Korea:** fans used K-pop objects in political celebration and protest, giving them meanings beyond concerts.
- **Fan chants:** practices created by fans now also circulate through official fan clubs managed by agencies.
- **Black Swan:** members from Senegal, Brazil, India and the United States trained within the K-pop system and performed in Korean.
- **Naevis:** an AI-generated virtual idol raises questions about nationality and the limits of the K-pop category.
