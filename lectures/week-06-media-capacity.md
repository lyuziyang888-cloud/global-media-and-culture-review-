# Week 6 Lecture: Media Capacity

## Central Purpose

The lecture uses Keane's framework to challenge Western-centric accounts of global media and evaluate how once-peripheral industries build media capacity.

## Lecture Structure

1. Media imperialism and the new international division of cultural labour often present Asian production mainly as cheap labour for Western companies.
2. This West-versus-Rest view can overlook the agency and development of non-Western media industries.
3. Changes in intellectual capital, co-production, market liberalisation and international interdependence create opportunities for new media centres.
4. Keane identifies five models: world factory, mimetic isomorphism, cultural technology transfer, niche breakthrough markets and creative/industrial clusters.
5. The first four models can produce success, but creative clusters provide the strongest basis for lasting capacity-building.
6. China's co-productions, use of global talent and regional streaming collaborations show how media capacity has changed since 2006.
7. Global integration can preserve inequalities while also creating opportunities for emergence.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

| Category | Short meaning | Lecture example |
|---|---|---|
| World factory | low-cost outsourced production; foreign companies may retain IP | Indian animation |
| Mimetic isomorphism | copying successful formats or formulas | *Hari Puttar*; Chinese drama clones |
| Cultural technology transfer | sharing expertise or IP through co-production, licensing and franchises | *Seven Samurai* / *The Magnificent Seven* |
| Niche breakthrough markets | distinctive content moves from niche circulation towards wider markets | *Hero*; *House of Flying Daggers* |
| Creative/industrial clusters | firms, infrastructure and skilled workers concentrate and exchange knowledge | Seoul Digital Media City |

## Main Arguments

- Western-centred theories can underestimate non-Western agency and industrial development.
- Participation in global production does not automatically create lasting media capacity.
- Keane's categories describe different routes from peripheral production towards greater capability.
- Creative and industrial clusters offer the strongest long-term model because knowledge, labour and infrastructure remain locally connected.
- Global integration can create new opportunities without eliminating unequal power.

## Cases and Evidence

- **Indian animation:** illustrates the world-factory model and limited control over intellectual property.
- ***Hari Puttar* and Chinese drama clones:** illustrate mimetic isomorphism.
- ***Seven Samurai* / *The Magnificent Seven*:** illustrate cultural technology transfer.
- ***Hero* and *House of Flying Daggers*:** illustrate niche breakthrough markets.
- **Seoul Digital Media City:** illustrates a creative and industrial cluster.

- Define and apply each of Keane's five categories.
- How has China's media capacity changed since 2006?
