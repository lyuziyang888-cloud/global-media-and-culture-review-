# Week 6 Lecture: Media Capacity

## Review Questions and Answers

### What are Western-centric approaches in global media studies? What are their shortcomings? What do we overlook when taking a Western-centric approach?

- Western-centric approaches use Western experience as the main model for understanding global media.
- They can overlook local industries, regional markets and different ways of developing media capacity.

### What is the purpose of Keane's framework? What does his framework critique?

- Keane's framework evaluates how regions develop lasting media capacity.
- It shows that cheap production or one international success does not necessarily create lasting capacity.

### Define, explain, and provide examples of each category of Keane's framework (in your own words)

- **De-territorialisation:** production moves to a cheaper location, such as foreign work outsourced to Asia.
- **Isomorphism:** producers copy a successful genre or business model, such as a local version of a proven format.
- **Cultural technology transfer:** knowledge enters through cooperation, such as workers learning through co-production.
- **Niche-market breakthrough:** a distinctive local work reaches audiences outside its original market.
- **Cultural/industrial milieu:** local firms and workers develop together in a connected production environment.

### How has China's media capacity changed since Keane discussed it in 2006?

- China is no longer mainly a low-cost production location.
- It now has a stronger domestic media industry and can produce more of its own content, although its global influence remains uneven.
