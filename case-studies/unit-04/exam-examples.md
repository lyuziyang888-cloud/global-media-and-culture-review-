# Unit 4 Exam Examples

| Concept | Example | Direct exam use |
|---|---|---|
| format scale | *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?* generated licensed adaptations in 115 territories | Proven concepts can be reproduced globally at large scale. |
| format package | production bibles and flying producers transfer budgets, schedules, casting advice and expertise | Formats circulate production knowledge, not merely finished content. |
| non-scripted format | *1 vs 100* preserves rules and set logic while adapting hosts and questions | Stable structures make non-scripted formats easier to reproduce. |
| scripted adaptation | *The Bridge* was adapted across several border regions | Drama requires more negotiation over narrative, place and cultural meaning. |
| contra-flow | *The Masked Singer* originated in South Korea and had 37 international adaptations | Format trade challenges a simple US-to-rest model. |
| diversified origins | formats from the "rest of the world" increased from 26% to 48% between 2004 and 2014 | Global format production became more geographically diverse. |
| format hubs | several national versions may be filmed in one location and localised through hosts and contestants | Production structures can be transnational while the screen text appears local. |
| streaming disruption | global streamers seek global rights rather than territory-by-territory licences | Platform expansion may weaken the traditional format-rights business model. |

## Best Three to Memorise

1. Production bible plus flying producer: knowledge transfer and risk reduction.
2. *The Masked Singer*: South Korean contra-flow.
3. 26% to 48%: diversification of format origins.
