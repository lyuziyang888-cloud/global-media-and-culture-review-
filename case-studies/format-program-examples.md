# Format Program Examples

## Why This Case Set Matters

These programs show how a stable format can be reproduced across markets while selected cultural and production elements change.

## 1 vs 100

- originated in the Netherlands
- non-scripted quiz format distributed to about 40 countries
- versions preserve the One-versus-Mob structure, logo, set logic, winning rules and increasing question difficulty
- local versions can adapt presentation style, hosts and questions

**Use it to explain:** non-scripted formats, standardisation, localisation and risk reduction.

## The Bridge

- originated as a Sweden-Denmark crime drama
- adapted for locations including UK-France, Malaysia-Singapore and Estonia-Russia
- requires narrative and cultural adaptation across borders

**Use it to explain:** scripted formats, geographical proximity and the greater cultural sensitivity of drama adaptation.

## Project Runway

- versions use different local hosts and production contexts
- retains the recognisable fashion-competition concept

**Use it to explain:** local casting within a globally licensed structure.

## The Traitors

- originated in the Netherlands and has UK, US, Australian and Indian versions
- raises the question of whether local versions are unique content or iterations of one underlying format
- can be used to test whether Dutch-origin format power challenges West-versus-Rest thinking

**Use it to explain:** format identity, local uniqueness, contra-flow and transnational production.

## Limitation

The PPT examples show adaptation and circulation but do not by themselves establish who owns the rights, receives licensing revenue or controls each production.
