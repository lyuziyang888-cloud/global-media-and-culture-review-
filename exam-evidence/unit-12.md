# Unit 12: Nollywood Evidence

## Marketers

**Use for:** Informality; distribution power (U2)

**Evidence:** Nollywood marketers finance films and control much of their physical distribution.

**What it proves:** Control over distribution gives intermediaries significant industry power.

## Bluetooth and Market Stalls

**Use for:** Alternative digital distribution; piracy

**Evidence:** Audiences use low-data file transfers and market stalls when streaming access is limited.

**What it proves:** Digital media can circulate without conventional streaming platforms.

## iROKOtv

**Use for:** Streaming localisation (U5); media capacity (U6)

**Evidence:** iROKOtv used kiosks, compressed files and locally suitable payment methods.

**What it proves:** A streaming service can adapt its model to local infrastructure and consumer needs.

## Netflix

**Use for:** Platform imperialism (U2); infrastructure

**Evidence:** Credit-card payments and data-heavy streaming do not suit every Nigerian audience.

**What it proves:** A standard global platform model may not work equally well in every market.

## Canal+ and Africa Magic

**Use for:** Formalisation; corporate power

**Evidence:** Formal rights agreements help Nollywood content circulate across the region.

**What it proves:** Wider circulation can also shift control towards large media companies.

**Source:** Miller (2021), pp. 259-276.
