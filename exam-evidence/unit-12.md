# Unit 12 Evidence-to-Concept Map

## Core Claim

Miller argues that formal streaming services do not automatically replace Nollywood's physical and informal distribution. Marketers, infrastructure limits and locally adapted file-sharing practices shape how Nigerian audiences access films.

## Evidence Details

### Marketers and Domestic Distribution

**Use for concepts:** distribution power, informality, industry structure

Marketers distribute physical copies, finance production and act as executive producers. Their private sales figures make profit sharing and outside investment difficult. Their collective market networks have also blocked large-scale attempts to replace them (pp. 260-262).

**What it proves:** Distribution power can matter as much as production. Informal networks can support the industry and protect existing gatekeepers at the same time.

### Bluetooth and Market Stalls

**Use for concepts:** alternative digital distribution, piracy, infrastructure

Miller describes villages where market stalls charged phones and transferred inexpensive unauthorised films through Bluetooth or FlashShare. The system required little data and only limited electricity (pp. 264-265).

**What it proves:** Digital distribution does not always mean an internet platform. Informal circulation can respond directly to local infrastructure.

### iROKOtv's Adaptation

**Use for concepts:** localisation, VOD, platform adaptation

iROKOtv experimented with cash or airtime payments at kiosks, offline downloads, compressed mobile files and person-to-person transfers requiring almost no data (pp. 265, 269).

**What it proves:** A digital service can change its technology and business model around users' conditions.

### Netflix's Limited Fit

**Use for concepts:** platform globalisation, cultural demand, infrastructure

Miller argues that Netflix retained credit-card payments and data-heavy streaming, while its library placed limited emphasis on Nigerian content. Local services already specialised in Nollywood for domestic and diaspora audiences (pp. 267-269).

**What it proves:** A global platform's established model may fit a local market poorly.

### Formalisation and Foreign Control

**Use for concepts:** formalisation, corporate power, media imperialism

Africa Magic and Canal+ created new routes for Nollywood across Africa. Miller also shows how formal contracts and corporate partnerships could bypass informal rights holders and strengthen larger foreign companies (pp. 270-272).

**What it proves:** Formal distribution may improve reach and investment while transferring control away from existing local networks.

## Limitation to Add

The chapter analyses a changing market and several platform experiments that later evolved or ended. Use it to explain the structure and tension between formal and informal distribution, then identify newer lecture examples separately.

## Exam-Ready Judgement

Nollywood challenges the assumption that digital modernisation must lead to platform streaming. Its hybrid system combines physical sales, informal file transfers and formal services. This improves local access, but power remains concentrated among marketers and increasingly contested by regional and global corporations.
