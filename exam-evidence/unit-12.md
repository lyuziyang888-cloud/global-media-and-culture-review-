## Evidence Details

### Marketers and Domestic Distribution

**Use for concepts:** distribution power, informality, industry structure

Marketers distribute physical copies, finance production and act as executive producers.

**What it proves:** Distribution power can matter as much as production.

### Bluetooth and Market Stalls

**Use for concepts:** alternative digital distribution, piracy, infrastructure

Miller describes villages where market stalls charged phones and transferred inexpensive unauthorised films through Bluetooth or FlashShare.

**What it proves:** Digital distribution does not always mean an internet platform.

### iROKOtv's Adaptation

**Use for concepts:** localisation, VOD, platform adaptation

iROKOtv tried kiosks, offline downloads, compressed files and transfers requiring almost no data (pp. 265, 269).

**What it proves:** A digital service can change its technology and business model around users' conditions.

### Netflix's Limited Fit

**Use for concepts:** platform globalisation, cultural demand, infrastructure

Miller argues that Netflix retained credit-card payments and data-heavy streaming, while its library placed limited emphasis on Nigerian content.

**What it proves:** A global platform's established model may fit a local market poorly.

### Formalisation and Foreign Control

**Use for concepts:** formalisation, corporate power, media imperialism

Africa Magic and Canal+ created new routes for Nollywood across Africa.

**What it proves:** Formal distribution may improve reach and investment while transferring control away from existing local networks.

## Exam-Ready Judgement

Nollywood challenges the assumption that digital modernisation must lead to platform streaming. Its hybrid system combines physical sales, informal file transfers and formal services. This improves local access, but power remains concentrated among marketers and increasingly contested by regional and global corporations.
