# Unit 5: Localising Streaming Evidence

## Main Claim

Rai argues that India is an industrial contact zone where Netflix and Disney+ Hotstar must negotiate with a large, culturally diverse and powerful local media industry. Platform power is therefore real but context-dependent.

## Best Evidence

### Hotstar's Cricketverse

**Use for concepts:** indigenized global, cultural proximity, local agency

Hotstar organised content around the IPL, Bollywood and Indian-language programming. The IPL attracted domestic, diasporic and transnational audiences; more than 700 million people watched it worldwide in 2018.

**What it proves:** Hotstar shows how global ownership can operate through an indigenized strategy built around local cultural and industrial strengths.

### Sacred Games

**Use for concepts:** cosmopolitan local, Bollywoodization

Netflix used an Indian story, Bollywood talent and crime-drama conventions familiar to global viewers; the series was promoted as India's *Narcos*.

**What it proves:** *Sacred Games* demonstrates that Netflix localises Indian production through globally recognisable genre logic while depending on Bollywood expertise.

### Netflix versus Hotstar

**Use for concepts:** industrial contact zones, platform imperialism

The reading reported approximately 25 million Hotstar subscribers in India, compared with 4.6 million for Netflix.

**What it proves:** This contrast complicates platform imperialism because a US platform's global scale does not guarantee dominance within every local market.

## Critical Limitation

Local adaptation and competition constrain Netflix, but they do not remove its global financial, technological and distribution power. Subscriber figures also describe a particular period rather than a permanent market position.

## Exam-Ready Judgement

India is an industrial contact zone in which global platforms and local industries reshape one another, although their resources and bargaining power remain unequal.

**Source:** Rai (2024), pp. 65-83, especially pp. 65-70.
