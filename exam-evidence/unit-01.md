# Unit 1: Global Media Evidence

## Main Claim

Canada's screen industry shows that local cultural identity and global circulation can coexist, but local media depends on production, representation, funding and regulation.

## Best Evidence

### Heated Rivalry

**Concepts:** local content, national identity, global/local

Canadian creators, crew, cast, locations and imagery make the program locally identifiable, while HBO gives it US reach.

**Use:** *Heated Rivalry* demonstrates that globally distributed content can retain a strong local identity because media nationality is constructed through production and representation, not distribution alone.

### North of North

**Concepts:** Indigenous representation, streaming platforms

The program is largely created, acted and produced by Indigenous Canadians, then distributed globally by Netflix.

**Use:** *North of North* shows that platforms can expand Indigenous visibility, although distribution power remains partly controlled by a global company.

### Canadian Streaming Levy

**Concepts:** media regulation, platform power

The CRTC proposed a 15% levy on major streamers' Canadian revenue to support domestic and Indigenous production.

**Use:** The proposed levy illustrates how governments try to make global platforms contribute to local cultural production.

## Critical Limitation

Global distribution does not automatically erase local identity, but visibility through a global platform does not give local producers full control over funding or circulation.

## Exam-Ready Judgement

Local and global media can coexist. The stronger question is which local elements remain visible and who controls production and distribution.
