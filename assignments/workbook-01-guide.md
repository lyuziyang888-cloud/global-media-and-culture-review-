# Assessment Task 1 Guide: Reusable Industry Insight Analysis Template

## Purpose

Use this template with any approved Industry Insight Report. It helps you compare media imperialism and cultural globalisation, apply at least two ideas from each paradigm, analyse report data and make a supported final judgement.

Do not submit this guide. Complete the planning fields, then convert the material into a continuous response in your own words.

### What the Feedback Means

The response identified relevant features of media imperialism and cultural globalisation, but it did not consistently show how a specific idea from Mirrlees explained a specific result from the selected report. Some claims also needed more precise figures, regions, time periods or indicators from the report.

For future responses, every analytical point should contain this complete chain:

```text
Mirrlees concept
-> exact report evidence
-> explanation of how the concept interprets that evidence
-> limitation of what the evidence can prove
-> effect on the final judgement
```

Do not write a paragraph about the reading and then a separate paragraph about the report. Integrate them sentence by sentence.

### Feedback-Derived Rules

1. Use at least two precise report figures, each with its measurement, region and period.
2. Place report evidence immediately after the Mirrlees idea it supports.
3. Explain the relationship using `because`, `shows`, `suggests` or another clear causal link.
4. Do not treat investment, production, availability and viewing as interchangeable evidence.
5. Do not claim audience popularity unless the report provides viewing or audience data.
6. Do not infer ownership, control or dependency unless the report provides evidence for it.
7. Compare the paradigms against the same report evidence before making the final judgement.

## Official Task Requirements

Your response must:

1. briefly explain the report's topic or industry trend
2. analyse how a media-imperialism scholar would interpret it
3. analyse how a cultural-globalisation scholar would interpret it

6. use no outside readings
7. argue which paradigm better explains the report

## Step 3: Choose Two Media-Imperialism Ideas

Choose the two ideas that fit the report best. Do not force every concept into the response.

| Possible idea | Meaning | Questions for application |
|---|---|---|
| foreign ownership or control | foreign media interests influence ownership, structure, distribution or content without equivalent reciprocal influence | Who owns the company or platform? Who makes commissioning and distribution decisions? |

## Step 5: Build the Comparative Argument

Complete this reasoning chain:

```text
The report demonstrates:

Media imperialism explains:

Cultural globalisation explains:

Media imperialism overlooks or underestimates:

Cultural globalisation overlooks or underestimates:

The stronger paradigm is:

It is stronger because:
```

A persuasive judgement does not need to claim that one paradigm is completely wrong. A qualified argument is usually stronger:

```text
[Paradigm A] explains [specific dimension] well. However, [Paradigm B]
provides the stronger overall explanation because [reason connected to
the report's evidence and the assessment question].
```

## Critical Analysis Checklist

Ask what the report measures and what it cannot establish.

- Does investment location reveal ownership?
- Does production location reveal who controls intellectual property?
- Does content availability prove that audiences watched it?
- Do viewing figures reveal revenue distribution?
- Does increased local production mean local producers gained decision-making power?
- Does one successful region prove that opportunities are globally equal?
- Does the report distinguish temporary disruption from a lasting structural change?
- Is the report describing media production, distribution, circulation, consumption or all four?
- Does the report have a commercial interest or narrow industry focus?

Use limitations carefully. Do not accuse the report of being wrong merely because it does not measure everything. Explain how its evidence supports some conclusions more strongly than others.

**Reusable critical sentence pattern:**

```text
The report measures [indicator] rather than [unmeasured dimension]. Its
evidence therefore demonstrates [supported conclusion] more clearly
than [less-supported conclusion].
```

## Paragraph Planning Template

### Planning Paragraph 1: Report and Thesis

```text
[Author] ([year]) examines [trend]. The report shows [key pattern or
statistic]. This change is associated with [reported cause]. Although
[competing interpretation], [chosen paradigm] provides the stronger
explanation because [main reason].
```

### Planning Paragraph 2: Media Imperialism

```text
A media-imperialism scholar would first emphasise [idea 1]. Mirrlees
([year], p. [page]) explains [paraphrased definition]. In the report,
[specific evidence] shows [analysis]. Second, [idea 2] helps explain
[specific evidence]. This matters because [mechanism or consequence].
```

### Planning Paragraph 3: Cultural Globalisation

```text
A cultural-globalisation scholar would instead emphasise [idea 1] and
[idea 2] (Mirrlees, [year], p. [page]). [Statistic or example] supports
this interpretation because [analysis]. However, [qualification based
on Mirrlees or a limitation of the evidence].
```

### Planning Paragraph 4: Comparison and Judgement

```text
[Paradigm A] explains [strength], but it underestimates [limitation].
The report measures [indicator] rather than [unmeasured dimension], so
it proves [supported claim] more clearly than [less-supported claim].
[Paradigm B] is ultimately stronger because [evidence-based reason].
```

Do not copy these sentence patterns unchanged. Replace every bracket and rewrite the language so it accurately reflects the selected report and your own argument.

## Evidence-to-Analysis Test

For each main point, check that the paragraph contains all four stages:

```text
Concept -> report evidence -> explanation of the connection -> significance for the final judgement
```

Description only states what happened. Analysis explains why a paradigm interprets that evidence in a particular way, what the evidence cannot prove and why one interpretation is more convincing.
