# Unit 3 Reading: Paradigms of Global Entertainment Media

## Source

Mirrlees, T. (2013). Paradigms of global entertainment media. In *Global Entertainment Media: Between Cultural Imperialism and Cultural Globalization*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203122747

## Central Argument

Neither cultural/media imperialism nor cultural globalisation perfectly explains every media flow. Mirrlees treats them as competing paradigms: the first foregrounds structural inequality and corporate power; the second foregrounds multidirectional exchange, audiences, hybridity and local meaning-making.

## Concepts and Evidence

| Concept | Exam-ready evidence | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| cultural imperialism | George Lucas argues that globally exported US entertainment carries American lifestyles and values | media flows can support Americanisation and homogenisation |
| cultural globalisation | Philippe Legrain describes globalisation as cultural exchange that enables new communities and hybrid cultures | audiences and cultures are not passive recipients |
| world-systems theory | core states and corporations benefit through unequal relations with semi-peripheral and peripheral zones | media industries operate within wider capitalist inequality |
| dependency theory | profits generated in peripheral markets may flow to Western corporations instead of supporting local development | market integration can reproduce underdevelopment |
| media imperialism | Boyd-Barrett focuses on pressure over ownership, structure, distribution or content without proportionate reciprocity | unequal influence can be analysed institutionally, not only textually |
| paradigm limits | Mirrlees notes that each perspective may fit some contexts but not all | conclusions must follow evidence rather than treating one theory as universal |

## Fast Exam Use

> Mirrlees shows that media imperialism is strongest when ownership, revenue and influence flow disproportionately toward dominant corporations, whereas cultural globalisation is stronger when evidence shows multidirectional flows, adaptation and active local meaning-making.

> Lucas and Legrain interpret the same cross-border circulation differently: Lucas stresses Americanisation, while Legrain stresses exchange, freedom and hybridity.
