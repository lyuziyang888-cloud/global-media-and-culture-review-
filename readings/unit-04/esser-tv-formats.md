# Unit 4 Reading: TV Formats

## Source

Esser, A. (2021). TV formats: Transnationalizing television production and distribution. In P. McDonald (Ed.), *The Routledge Companion to Media Industries* (pp. 351-362). Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780429275340-35

## Central Argument

Esser argues that format trade has transnationalised television by creating cross-border production and distribution structures, circulating production knowledge and multiplying the direction and complexity of program flows.

## Concepts and Evidence

| Concept | Exam-ready evidence | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| format program | Chalaby defines formats as licensed remakes, adaptable recipes, proven concepts and shared production methods | formats transfer both intellectual property and know-how |
| risk reduction | production bibles provide schedules, budgets, casting and marketing guidance; flying producers provide expertise | tested knowledge makes production faster and less risky |
| standardisation/localisation | licensors protect brand identity while licensees negotiate changes for local needs | global consistency and local adaptation occur together |
| contra-flow | formats originating in the "rest of the world" rose from 26% in 2004 to 48% in 2014 | format flows are not simply US-to-rest |
| non-Western format power | South Korea's *The Masked Singer* had 37 adaptations, with another 13 reported as forthcoming | peripheral or regional producers can become global format originators |
| transnational production | format hubs film several national versions in one location while local hosts and contestants differentiate them | production can be transnational even when content appears local |
| platform power | global streamers seek global rights, potentially weakening territorial licensing revenue | streaming can disrupt the format industry's established business model |

## Fast Exam Use

> *The Masked Singer* challenges one-way-flow models because a South Korean format generated dozens of adaptations across multiple continents.

> Production bibles and flying producers show that formats circulate production expertise, not merely finished television texts.
