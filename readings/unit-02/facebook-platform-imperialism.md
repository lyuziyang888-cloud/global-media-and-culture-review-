# Unit 2 Reading: Facebook's Platform Imperialism

## Source

Jin, D. Y. (2019). Facebook's platform imperialism: The economics and geopolitics of social media. In O. Boyd-Barrett & T. Mirrlees (Eds.), *Media Imperialism: Continuity and Change* (pp. 187-198). Rowman & Littlefield.

## Central Argument

Jin argues that Facebook is a platform capitalist and platform imperialist whose global expansion strengthens asymmetrical economic, technological, political and cultural relations between the United States and other countries.

## Concepts and Evidence

| Concept | Exam-ready evidence | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| platform capitalism | Facebook turns user attention and data into targeted advertising revenue | apparently free participation supports private capital accumulation |
| data commodification | users voluntarily communicate while unintentionally producing commercially valuable data | user activity becomes a commodity |
| platform imperialism | Jin defines it as asymmetrical interdependence involving unequal technological exchange and capital flows | digital platforms renew media imperialism rather than ending it |
| network effects | Facebook was the largest social-network site in 152 of 167 analysed countries in January 2018 | scale makes US platform dominance difficult for local rivals to counter |
| geopolitics | US political actors support Facebook's expansion while the platform can support surveillance and political campaigns | corporate and state power are interconnected |

## Fast Exam Use

> Facebook illustrates platform imperialism because global users produce data and advertising value, while ownership, infrastructure and capital accumulation remain concentrated in a US corporation.

> Facebook's horizontal communication between users can conceal the vertical power of the company that owns the infrastructure, shapes participation and monetises data.
