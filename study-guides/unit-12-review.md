# Unit 12 Review: Nollywood and Informality

**Unit files:** [Concepts](../concepts/unit-12-nollywood-informality/) | [Lecture](../lectures/week-12-nollywood-informality.md) | [Tutorial](../tutorials/week-12-nollywood.md) | [Evidence](../exam-evidence/unit-12.md)

## Must Know

- **Nollywood:** Nigeria's film industry, historically based on straight-to-video production.
- **Informality:** organised exchange through trust and personal networks rather than complete formal systems.
- **Marketers:** funders and distributors who control much of Nollywood's physical market.
- **Alternative digital distribution:** Bluetooth, USB, kiosks and downloads that work around infrastructure limits.

## Best Examples

- **Bluetooth market stalls:** films move phone to phone with little or no internet access.
- **iROKOtv:** used kiosks, compressed downloads and low-data sharing to adapt distribution.
- **Netflix:** retained a conventional payment and streaming model and offered limited Nigerian content in Miller's case.
- **Marketers:** their domestic distribution networks remained essential even for many higher-budget films.

## Official Tutorial Exam Practice

### How does Nollywood challenge Global North ideas of digital distribution?

Nollywood shows that digital distribution can occur through Bluetooth, USB and offline downloads rather than continuous streaming. These practices respond to high data prices and unreliable electricity. Marketers still control the physical networks that reach domestic audiences, so digital and physical distribution coexist. This model improves access but can limit transparency and creators' control.

## Discussion Questions

### Is informality a weakness?

Not entirely. It supports flexible and affordable circulation, but limited records and contracts can weaken transparency, investment and producers' rights.

### Why may Netflix struggle while iROKOtv adapts?

iROKOtv focused on Nollywood content and experimented with compressed downloads and low-data access. Miller found that Netflix used a more standard global model and offered relatively little Nigerian content.

### Who defines global success?

Platforms may use international reach, while creators may value control and income. Domestic audiences may judge success through access and cultural relevance.

## Exam Answer Pattern

```text
define the concept
-> identify the Western assumption
-> explain the Nollywood alternative
-> apply one specific example
-> add the marketer or infrastructure issue

```

**Useful judgement:** Nollywood does not represent an incomplete version of Western streaming. It demonstrates a different distribution system shaped by local infrastructure, audience demand and existing market power.
