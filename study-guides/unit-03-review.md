# Unit 3 Review: Cultural Globalisation

## Must Know

- **Cultural globalisation:** media and culture move across borders in multiple directions and change through local interpretation.
- **Cultural proximity:** audiences often prefer familiar languages and cultures.
- **Contra-flow:** media moves from outside the traditional US or Western centre.
- **Media capitals:** cities become regional centres for producing and circulating media.

## Best Examples

- ***Jane the Virgin*:** a US adaptation of the Venezuelan format *Juana la virgen*.
- **Chinese microdramas:** Chinese-origin content and production models reaching global audiences.
- **National Netflix Top 10 lists:** show that viewing differs across countries.

## Tutorial Exam Practice

### What is cultural globalisation?

It explains multidirectional media flows, active audiences, local adaptation and non-US media power.

### Why is it important?

It corrects the idea that media only flows from the US and that audiences passively accept foreign culture.

### What is a precise example?

*Jane the Virgin* demonstrates contra-flow and adaptation because it remade a Venezuelan telenovela for a US audience.

## Discussion Questions

### Do Chinese microdramas challenge media imperialism?

Yes, because Chinese content travels globally. However, global platforms may still control visibility, data and revenue.

### Which paradigm is better?

Cultural globalisation better explains movement and adaptation. Media imperialism better explains unequal ownership and distribution.

## Exam Answer Pattern

```text
define cultural globalisation
-> explain multidirectional flow or adaptation
-> apply one example
-> mention structural inequality
-> give a judgement
```

**Useful judgement:** Cultural globalisation explains cultural movement and local agency, but media imperialism is still needed to explain unequal industrial power.
