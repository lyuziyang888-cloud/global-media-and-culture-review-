# Unit 11 Review: K-pop Fandom

**Unit files:** [Concepts](../concepts/unit-11-k-pop-fandom/) | [Tutorial](../tutorials/week-11-k-pop-fandom.md) | [Evidence](../exam-evidence/unit-11.md)

## Must Know

- **Cultural hybridity:** fusion of cultural forms that can also disrupt fixed cultural categories.
- **Participatory globalisation:** fans experience globalisation by creating, adapting and circulating media.
- **Cosmopolitan globalisation:** fans imagine cultural flow beyond one national or Western centre.
- **Transcultural fandom:** shared interests, emotions and identities connect fans across national borders.

## Best Examples

- **KATSEYE:** combines K-pop industrial practices with a multinational group and global pop strategy.
- **Cover dance:** fans reproduce recognisable choreography while changing performers, place and presentation.
- **Melbourne Taemin banner:** fans combined K-pop support culture with Australian animals, landscape and language.
- **Fan labour:** participation gives fans agency but can also create value for agencies and platforms.

## Official Tutorial Exam Practice

### How does KATSEYE demonstrate cultural hybridity?

Its industrial strategy combines K-pop training and performance conventions with multinational members and global pop positioning. This challenges a fixed Korean-versus-Western division, although major companies still organise and profit from the mixture.

## Discussion Questions

### Are cover dances hybridity or copying?

They may copy core choreography and still become hybrid through changes in place, performers and performance style. Use details from the selected video before deciding.

### Why is K-pop fandom transcultural?

Fans connect around shared music and emotion across national borders. Nationality remains relevant, but it is not the only identity shaping the community.

**Useful judgement:** K-pop fandom allows active cross-border adaptation, but participation operates within media systems still shaped by agencies and platforms.
