# Unit 10 Review: Cosmopolitanism and Turkish Dramas

**Unit files:** [Concepts](../concepts/unit-10-cosmopolitanism/) | [Tutorial](../tutorials/week-10-cosmopolitanism.md) | [Evidence](../exam-evidence/unit-10.md) | [Lecture](../lectures/week-10-cosmopolitanism-and-dramas.md)

## Must Know

- **Cosmopolitanism:** openness to cultural difference and a sense of belonging to a wider world.
- **Banal cosmopolitanism:** foreign culture becomes part of ordinary media consumption.
- **Pop cosmopolitanism:** active engagement with foreign popular culture develops knowledge and reflection.
- **Cultural proximity:** audiences often prefer content that feels culturally familiar.

## Best Examples

- **Amparo:** watched Turkish dramas to learn how other people live, even when she disagreed with what she saw.
- **Active fans:** followed Turkish media, studied the language, joined communities or travelled to Turkey.
- **Critical viewers:** recognised that wealthy Istanbul stories did not represent the whole country and questioned gender inequality.
- **Spanish broadcasters:** imported Turkish dramas because they were affordable and repeated well, then used remakes across streaming and television.

## Official Tutorial Exam Practice

### How does cosmopolitanism challenge cultural proximity?

Cultural proximity explains appeal through familiarity, such as melodrama and shared moral themes. Cosmopolitanism explains why difference also attracts viewers. Spanish audiences showed curiosity about Turkish places and customs, and some researched or travelled beyond the programs. The strongest judgement is that familiarity and difference worked together.

## Discussion Questions

### Does watching foreign media make someone cosmopolitan?

Not automatically. Watching can create everyday exposure, but stronger cosmopolitanism requires openness, learning or critical reflection.

### Why were Turkish dramas popular in Spain?

Broadcasters valued their cost and repeat potential; audiences valued melodrama, novelty and cultural difference. Availability and audience motivation worked together.

## Exam Answer Pattern

```text
define cultural proximity and cosmopolitanism
-> state how they differ
-> use one viewer example
-> add the industrial context
-> identify the sample or representation limitation
-> judge how the explanations work together
```

**Useful judgement:** Turkish dramas show that audiences may seek cultural difference, but access, familiar genres and prior cosmopolitan experiences still shape what they watch.
