# Unit 6 Review: Media Capacity

## Must Know

- **Media capacity:** the ability to create, finance, own, improve and circulate media, not simply perform low-cost work.
- **Western-centric approach:** treats Western experience and power as the universal model and may overlook non-Western agency.
- **Keane's five models:** world factory, mimetic isomorphism, cultural technology transfer, niche breakthrough markets and creative/industrial clusters.

## Best Examples

- **India's animation industry:** outsourcing creates work and skills, but foreign owners may retain IP.
- ***Hero* and *House of Flying Daggers*:** culturally distinctive films entering wider markets.
- **Seoul Digital Media City:** infrastructure and talent concentrated in a creative cluster.
- **China:** co-productions, global talent and regional streaming partnerships show changing capacity.

## Key Questions

### Why does Keane criticise West-versus-Rest thinking?

It focuses on Western dominance and can overlook how non-Western industries use global integration to develop skills, IP, infrastructure and market power.

### Which model represents the strongest capacity-building?

Creative/industrial clusters. They combine firms, workers, infrastructure and knowledge exchange, making repeated innovation more possible than one outsourced job or global hit.

### Does global integration always disadvantage peripheral producers?

No. Unequal power remains, but integration can also create opportunities for emerging producers. The result depends on who owns IP and whether local capability continues to grow.

**Useful judgement:** Global visibility is not the same as media capacity; capacity depends on lasting control over production, knowledge, intellectual property and circulation.
