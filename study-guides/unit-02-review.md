# Unit 2 Review: Media Imperialism

## Must Know

- **Media imperialism:** powerful countries or corporations dominate media production, distribution and infrastructure in weaker markets.
- **One-way flow:** media mainly moves from powerful centres to dependent markets.
- **Platform imperialism:** platforms extend this power through data, algorithms, infrastructure and market access.

```text
concentrated ownership
-> control of production and distribution
-> unequal media flows
-> pressure on local industries and culture
```

## Best Examples

- **Facebook:** controls data, visibility and access to news audiences.
- **Australian news block:** shows that a platform can pressure a national government.
- **Hollywood-Pentagon cooperation:** entertainment can support US military ideas.

## Tutorial Discussion Questions

### Does the US influence global media?

Yes. US companies have major financial and distribution power, but audiences and local industries still have agency.

### Can local industries resist?

Yes, through local languages, strong audiences, funding and regulation. However, they may still depend on global platforms.

### Is media imperialism still relevant?

Yes for explaining structural power. It is weaker at explaining active audiences and successful non-US media.

### Can non-US companies exercise imperial power?

Yes. Imperialism is about unequal control, not only whether the company is American.

### Can regulation protect local media?

Yes, but powerful platforms can lobby, withdraw services or pressure governments.

## Exam Answer Pattern

```text
define media imperialism
-> explain ownership or infrastructure power
-> apply one example
-> give one limitation
-> decide whether the theory still works
```

**Useful judgement:** Media imperialism remains useful for analysing ownership and platform power, but it should not treat audiences as passive or global flows as completely one-way.
